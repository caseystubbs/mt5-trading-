# Recent Real-World Performance of Systematic FX Strategies, FX Products, and Retail FX Traders (2010–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: out-of-sample, real-money or investable evidence for 2010–2026. Anything from before 2010 is labelled **[Background]**. Each fact has an inline source. Where a figure could not be verified it is listed under Gaps rather than estimated.

## Q1. How have investable FX factor products, indices, and professional currency managers performed since 2010?

### Takeaway
The flagship investable G10 carry product, Invesco DB G10 Currency Harvest (DBV), was liquidated in March 2023 because it was too small to cover its costs. Its underlying index never regained its July 2007 peak at any point through end-2022. Dedicated currency managers shrank sharply after 2008: FX Concepts went from a $12bn peak to closing in 2013, and the Parker FX Index tracked only 42 programmes by 2013. Currency-manager indices since then show small, inconsistent returns, with only occasional good years such as 2024.

### Cited Findings
**Invesco DB G10 Currency Harvest Fund (DBV)**
- Strategy: the index goes long futures in the 3 highest-yielding G10 currencies and short the 3 lowest-yielding. It rebalances quarterly, with notional-to-collateral of about 2:1 (about 1.66:1 when USD is in the top or bottom set) — [DBV 10-K FY2022, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000095017023010813/dbv-20221231.htm)
- The DB G10 Currency Future Harvest Index (Excess Return) has been calculated since 12 Mar 1993 from a base of USD 100. Through 31 Dec 2022 its range was **high USD 315.27 on 25 Jul 2007** and low USD 94.03 on 30 Jul 1993. In other words, the index set no new high in the 15+ years from 2008 to 2022 — [DBV 10-K FY2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000095017023010813/dbv-20221231.htm)
- Fees: 0.75% per year management fee plus about 0.03% per year brokerage — [DBV 10-K FY2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000095017023010813/dbv-20221231.htm)
- Shares outstanding: 1,300,000 as of 31 Jan 2023 — [DBV 10-K FY2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000095017023010813/dbv-20221231.htm)
- Closure timeline:
  - **23 Jan 2023:** the board approved a plan to liquidate.
  - **24 Feb 2023:** creations stopped after the close.
  - **3 Mar 2023:** last trading day on NYSE Arca.
  - **About 10 Mar 2023:** remaining assets liquidated and cash distributed.
  - Sources: [DBV 10-K FY2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000095017023010813/dbv-20221231.htm); [Prospectus Supplement 424B3, 23 Jan 2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000119312523013247/d407214d424b3.htm)
- Stated reason: given the Fund's net assets relative to its operating expenses, the Managing Owner found it "unreasonable and imprudent to continue the Fund's business." No dollar AUM figure is given — [424B3, 23 Jan 2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1354730/000119312523013247/d407214d424b3.htm)

**Deutsche Bank Currency Returns (DBCR) index — carry + momentum + valuation [mostly Background]**
- DBCR combines three strategies: carry, momentum and valuation — [Merk Investments glossary](https://legacy.merkinvestments.com/insights/glossary/dbcr.php)
- **[Background, pre-2010 sample]** In a DWS/db x-trackers marketing newsletter dated 2 Nov 2010, DBCR (GBP hedged) showed 8.2% annualised return and 5.5% annualised volatility over 2000–2010. The related UCITS ETF launched 29 Jul 2010 — [DWS Currency Returns newsletter](https://etf.dws.com/en-gb/AssetDownload/Index/dd4c3935-e9a9-4b80-ab2f-350acc851cf3/Newsletter-Currency-Returns.pdf)

**Commentary on G10 carry after 2008**
- Commerzbank, cited in Euromoney on 2 Nov 2012: "unleveraged G10 carry trade returns since Q2 2009" were 4.9% in USD terms and 9.4% in EUR terms. The article does not say whether these are cumulative or annualised. The carry basket kept the same pairs (AUD-CHF, JPY-NOK, USD-SEK) for 7 straight quarters to Q2 2012. Since 1990 only one other period (1997) had the basket unchanged for more than 5 quarters. Average G10 rate differentials were 0.25pp lower than before the crisis — [Euromoney, "Foreign exchange: Is the carry dead or alive?"](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh0njicsw/capital-markets/foreign-exchange-is-the-carry-dead-or-alive/)
- Challenger (1 Dec 2022): a simple monthly G10 strategy long the top-4 and short the bottom-4 yielders "performed well before Lehman and poorly after." The article gives the result in charts only, with no numbers. It says G10 carry levels in late 2022 were back to pre-Lehman levels for the first time — [Challenger, "Carry makes a comeback"](https://www.legacy.challenger.com.au/institutional/insights/carry-makes-a-comeback)
- Man Group: FX and fixed-income carry were profitable in 2023 because "higher-for-longer" US rates favoured positions against low-rate currencies such as JPY — [Man Group, "A Trend Following Deep Dive: The Dynamics of Dispersion"](https://www.man.com/insights/deep-dive-trend-following)

**Professional currency managers / currency hedge funds**
- **FX Concepts** (John Taylor), as of Euromoney, 21 Oct 2013:
  - Founded in 1981.
  - Peak AUM of **$12bn** during 2000–2008.
  - Flagship Global Currency Programme returned about 16% over 2000–2008 but was only **about +3% since inception** by Oct 2013, and **−10% YTD in 2013**.
  - AUM had fallen to **$621m**, and the firm was returning money and closing all five funds.
  - Source: [Euromoney, "FX Concepts closure signals shift in FX investing"](https://www.euromoney.com/?p=66955)
  - The wind-down was confirmed on 9 Oct 2013 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2013/10/09/john-taylors-fx-concepts-confirms-wind-down.html) (headline/URL only, not fully read)
  - The holding company later filed for bankruptcy after its last client left — [Finance Magnates](https://financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/technology/fx-concepts-holding-company-files-for-bankruptcy-following-outflow-of-final-client/amp) (headline only, date not verified)
- **Parker FX Index** (2013): −2.57% YTD to end-Sep 2013, tracking **42 programmes run by 35 firms** worldwide. Its systematic programmes lost 3.87% over May–Jul 2013 and stood at −0.78% YTD — [Euromoney, 21 Oct 2013](https://www.euromoney.com/?p=66955)
- **Barclay BTOP FX Index:** −1.08% for 2013 (as of Oct 2013) — [Euromoney, 21 Oct 2013](https://www.euromoney.com/?p=66955)
- **Barclay Currency Traders Index:**
  - 2012 full year: **+1.59%**, versus the Barclay CTA Index at −1.64% and the Systematic Traders Index at −3.11% — [Opalesque/BarclayHedge, 15 Jan 2013](https://www.opalesque.com/industry-updates/3075/barclay-cta-index-gains-in-managed.html)
  - Aug 2024: **+1.57%**, bringing **YTD 2024 to +5.57%**. The Barclay CTA Index was +2.92% YTD at the same point. The currency index was described as the "top performing TradFi sub-sector" — [The Full FX, 24 Sep 2024](https://thefullfx.com/?p=5244)

### Inferences
- An investable G10 carry index never recovering its 2007 high in 15+ years is the cleanest real-world out-of-sample result in this set: on an excess-return basis, the classic G10 carry factor delivered roughly nothing to negative from 2008 to 2022. The fund then closed just as rate differentials began to widen again in 2022–23.
- The pattern is "industry shrinks, survivors earn low single digits": dedicated currency managers went from a multi-billion-dollar niche to a few dozen programmes by 2013. Long-run professional currency alpha after 2008 looks weak and episodic, not persistent.
- 2024's strong currency-manager year fits a regime view: FX strategies do better when rate dispersion and volatility are high (2022–2024), and badly in zero-rate, low-dispersion regimes (about 2009–2021).

### Gaps
- **DBV:** annual NAV returns for 2010–2022, AUM at liquidation, and the index level at 31 Dec 2022 were not found. The 10-K text read did not include the financial tables.
- **DBCR and its carry/momentum/valuation sub-indices:** no verified post-2010 return figures. The current status of the DBCR UCITS ETF (open or closed) was not verified.
- **Barclays, J.P. Morgan and Credit Suisse FX carry or FX momentum indices:** no verified performance data found.
- **Barclay Currency Traders Index:** year-by-year returns for 2010–2025 and the number of constituent programmes over time were not retrieved. BarclayHedge pages now redirect to ION Analytics.
- **Parker FX Index:** no post-2013 data was found, and whether the index still exists was not verified.
- **QFS:** a search headline ("qfs shutting down currency hedge fund", [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/qfs-shutting-down-currency-hedge-fund/)) suggests QFS closed a currency hedge fund, but the date and details were not verified.

## Q2. How has CTA/trend following performed since 2009, and what has FX trend contributed?

### Takeaway
Diversified trend following had a long weak stretch after the global financial crisis (Man calls it the "CTA winter"). Since 2000 the SG Trend Index has compounded only about 5.2% a year, with a 20.6% maximum drawdown. Returns are concentrated in a few big years, such as 2014 (+19.7%) and 2022 (+27.35%). No public source found quantifies FX's share of trend P&L for 2010–2025. Recent commentary repeatedly calls currency markets "directionless."

### Cited Findings
**SG Trend Index**
- 2022: **+27.35%**, its best year since launch in Jan 2000 and only the third year above +20%. The previous double-digit year was **2014 at +19.7%** — [The Full FX, 16 Jan 2023](https://thefullfx.com/?p=3378)
- 2025: **+2.39%** for the full year, with December +1.92% — [Top Traders Unplugged, Dec 2025 report, published 23 Jan 2026](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/?p=20381)
- Since 2000: compound annual growth of **5.23%** and maximum drawdown of **20.61%**. In Oct 2025, "currency markets remained largely directionless" — [Top Traders Unplugged, Oct 2025 report](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/?p=19314)
- Annual standard deviation since Dec 1998 is 13.6%. March 2023 was its second-worst five-day drawdown since Jan 2000 — [Man Group, "Trend following: rolling with the punches"](https://www.man.com/insights/trend-following-rolling-with-punches)

**SG CTA Index**
- 2022: **+20.13%**, a record and its first double-digit year since **2014 (+15.66%)** — [The Full FX, 16 Jan 2023](https://thefullfx.com/?p=3378)

**Man Group commentary (Man AHL / Man Systematic)**
- Data for 2000–2024: dispersion between trend managers rose after 2007. Post-GFC dispersion came mainly from the choice of markets traded, with "traditional trend struggling in the 'CTA winter'" while alternative markets did well. The article gives no FX-specific sector numbers.
- Long-USD positions were among those that outperformed in the 2002 and 2008 crises.
- Source: [Man Group, "A Trend Following Deep Dive: The Dynamics of Dispersion"](https://www.man.com/insights/deep-dive-trend-following) (also in [AIMA Journal](https://www.aima.org/article/a-trend-following-deep-dive-the-dynamics-of-dispersion.html))

### Inferences
- A diversified, institutional-grade trend index across about 100+ futures markets made about 5% a year over 2000–2025 and needed rare crisis or inflation years to get there. A single-asset-class FX trend system with 8 currencies, weekly bars and retail costs has far less diversification. Weak or negative results over 2000–2026 are consistent with this evidence and are not necessarily a coding error.
- Because FX contribution is never cited as a driver of recent trend wins (2022 was rates and commodities driven, and 2025 commentary calls FX directionless), FX trend was probably a minor P&L source after 2009. This is unconfirmed by numbers (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No public source quantified the **currency sector's contribution** to the SG Trend or SG CTA indices for any year 2010–2025, or FX trend Sharpe by sub-period. AQR's "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following" and Man/AHL sector attribution charts were not retrieved.
- SG Trend and SG CTA annual returns for 2010–2013, 2015–2021, 2023 and 2024 were not retrieved as exact figures. Man shows them only in chart images.

## Q3. What does BIS research say about the profitability of FX strategies after 2008, and about the August 2024 yen carry unwind?

### Takeaway
BIS research treats FX strategies mainly as **tail-risk carriers**. Carry, momentum and term-spread strategies have substantial crash risk, and their behaviour in market stress is inconsistent. In August 2024, roughly ¥40 trillion (about $250bn) of carry trades were "hit hard" by procyclical deleveraging. The BIS Triennial Survey measures turnover, not strategy returns, so it does not answer the profitability question.

### Cited Findings
- **BIS Bulletin No. 90** (27 Aug 2024; Aquilina, Lombardi, Schrimpf, Sushko):
  - Volatility returned in early August after a negative US macro release.
  - Leveraged positions in equities and currencies were unwound, amplifying the move.
  - FX carry trades "were hit hard by the deleveraging pressures."
  - FX carry trade size going into the event: a rough "middle ballpark of **¥40 trillion ($250 billion)**," described as "if anything, biased down due to data gaps."
  - Markets stabilised quickly and outright market dysfunction was avoided.
  - Source: [BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.htm)
- BIS Quarterly Review (16 Sep 2024): the BIS treats the 5 Aug 2024 unwind as part of a broader deleveraging that goes beyond yen-funded carry, with multi-strategy hedge funds spreading stress across asset classes — [FX Markets, 17 Sep 2024](https://www.fx-markets.com/trading/7949395/bis-says-carry-trade-unwind-is-part-of-broader-pattern)
- BIS Quarterly Review, Dec 2011 (Gyntelberg & Schrimpf, "FX strategies in periods of distress"): multi-currency carry, momentum and term-spread strategies "carry substantial tail risk," and their performance is "not uniform across periods of global market distress." Equity investments showed even greater downside risk — [BIS QR Dec 2011](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1112e.htm)
- **[Background]** BIS QR Sep 2007, "Evidence of carry trade activity" — [BIS](https://bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt0709e.htm) (not read in detail)

### Inferences
- August 2024 is a live, out-of-sample example of the textbook carry profile: years of small positive carry, then a sharp loss in days when leverage unwinds. Any carry component in a G8 system needs explicit crash and volatility controls.
- No BIS publication found here claims FX factor strategies have been reliably profitable after 2008. The BIS emphasis is on risk and market stability, not returns.

### Gaps
- Exact USD/JPY and MXN/JPY moves and carry-index drawdown figures for Jul–Aug 2024 were not verified. The BIS Bulletin 90 PDF and the Sep 2024 Quarterly Review full text did not return the numbers.
- No BIS Triennial Survey publication on **strategy profitability** was found. The Triennial covers turnover and market structure.
- The numerical results (Sharpe ratios, drawdowns) of the Dec 2011 BIS article were not extracted.

## Q4. What are the documented outcomes for retail FX/CFD traders?

### Takeaway
Every regulator dataset and mandated broker disclosure found shows a large majority of retail FX/CFD accounts losing money:
- **EU:** 74–89% of accounts lose (ESMA standard warning).
- **UK:** 82% lost (FCA, 2016).
- **France:** 89% lost over four years, an average of about €10,900 each (AMF, 2009–2012).
- **US:** only about 33–37% of accounts profitable in any quarter across NFA dealers (2013–2015). The best US broker, Interactive Brokers, reports about 44–47% profitable in 2025–2026.

Academic data on a large retail FX social network shows traders were unprofitable on average and traded more after seeing peers' gains.

### Cited Findings
**EU – ESMA**
- Mandated risk warning under ESMA's product intervention: "Between **74-89%** of retail investor accounts lose money when trading CFDs." Each provider must also state its own percentage, recalculated every 3 months over the preceding 12 months.
- ESMA adopted the decisions on 22 May 2018. CFD restrictions applied from 1 Aug 2018 and the binary options ban from 2 Jul 2018.
- Source: [ESMA notice of product intervention decisions (ESMA35-43-1135)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma35-43-1135_notice_of_pi_decisions_on_cfds_and_binary_options.pdf)

**UK – FCA**
- FCA analysis of "a representative sample of client accounts for CFD firms" found **82%** of clients lost money — [FCA press release, 6 Dec 2016](https://fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-proposes-stricter-rules-contract-difference-products)

**France – AMF [straddles 2010]**
- Study of about **15,000** retail Forex/CFD clients over **2009–2012**:
  - **89%** lost money (13,224 losers vs 1,575 winners).
  - Average loss of about **€10,900** per losing client.
  - Net losses of about €161m (about €175m of losses against €13.8m of gains).
  - The published figures do not fully reconcile: 13,224 of 15,000 is about 88%.
- Source: [La finance pour tous, 13 Oct 2014](https://www.lafinancepourtous.com/Actualites/Forex-attention-danger); primary: [AMF Observatoire de l'épargne letter no. 10, Oct 2014](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/lettre_ou_cahier/lettre_observatoire/La%20lettre%20de%20l%27Observatoire%20de%20l%27epargne%20de%20l%27AMF%20-%20ndeg%2010%20-%20Octobre%202014.pdf) (not opened directly)

**Global broker disclosures**
- Among the 20 largest retail brokers that publish client P/L ratios, about **25%** of clients were profitable in Aug 2023, so about 75% lost.
- Broker-level profitable shares: TeleTrade 35%, Saxo 35%, Hantec 29.38%, Tickmill 29%, OANDA 29%, XM 24.67%, XTB 23%, Pepperstone 18.8%, Plus500 18%, Axi 17.3%.
- Source: [Finance Magnates, 25 Sep 2023](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/analysis-75-of-retail-investors-are-essentially-capital-donors/)

**US – CFTC/NFA mandated disclosures**
- Forex dealer members must state the number of non-discretionary retail forex accounts and the percentage profitable and unprofitable each quarter. The rules are CFTC Regulation 5.5(b) (risk disclosure) and 5.5(e) (quarterly disclosure), with profitability calculated under Reg 5.18(i). Dealers must post the 4 most recent quarters on their websites — [NFA Forex Regulatory Guide](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/member-resources/files/forex-regulatory-guide.html)
- Q4 2013, all US retail FX: **33.6%** of 96,587 trading accounts profitable (Q3 2013: 33.4%). Interactive Brokers 45.3% of 24,935; OANDA 32.5%; GAIN Capital/FOREX.com 27% of 10,819 — [Finance Magnates, 27 Jan 2014](https://financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/exclusive-q4-us-retail-forex-profitability-report-ib-1-again-while-oanda-slipping)
- Q4 2015: weighted average **37.4%** profitable (Q3 2015: 34.9%) across 89,478 active US accounts. Interactive Brokers 45% of 32,956; OANDA 37.3% — [Finance Magnates, Q4 2015 report](https://financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/exclusive-q4-2015-us-retail-forex-profitability-report-shows-improved-trading/amp)
- Interactive Brokers' own NFA-required disclosure (including commissions):

  | Quarter | Profitable | Accounts |
  |---|---|---|
  | Q4 2025 | 43.93% | 23,895 |
  | Q1 2026 | 46.09% | 23,840 |
  | Q2 2026 | 45.53% | 23,358 |
  | Q3 2026 | **46.50%** | 23,094 |

  Source: [Interactive Brokers, Performance of Retail Client Forex Accounts](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/index.php?f=3731)

**Academic studies of retail FX traders**
- **Heimer & Simon, "Facebook Finance: How Social Interaction Propagates Active Investing"** (Cleveland Fed WP 15-22, 2015)
  - Data: a social network for retail FX traders (pseudonym "myForexBook") linked to 53 brokers. Jan 2009 – early Dec 2010. 3,117 traders (filtered), 111,928 trader-weeks, about 2.15m positions.
  - Mean **weekly return −0.028** (sd 0.20). Mean leverage 8.6x. Mean 25.54 trades per week (median 11).
  - The authors say this group loses more after compounding than Barber–Odean stock investors (about −0.05 per month), mainly because of leverage.
  - About one-third of US accounts were profitable, per CFTC quarterly reports.
  - Positive peer returns raised a trader's activity by about 5 trades per week (about 20%). Negative peer returns had no detectable effect.
  - Network members' trading rose from about 20 to about 30 trades per week, and portfolio volatility rose by up to 25%, with no rise in profitability.
  - Source: [Cleveland Fed WP 15-22 PDF](https://www.clevelandfed.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/clevelandfedsite/publications/working-papers/2015/wp-1522-facebook-finance-social-interaction-investing-pdf.pdf)
- **Heimer, "Peer Pressure: Social Interaction and the Disposition Effect"** (Cleveland Fed WP 16-18, 2016): access to the social trading network "nearly doubles the magnitude of a trader's disposition effect," and connected traders develop correlated disposition effects — [Cleveland Fed WP 16-18](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/working-paper/2016/wp-1618-peer-pressure-social-interaction-and-the-disposition-effect)

### Inferences
- The base rate is clear: in any given quarter, roughly 1 in 4 to 1 in 3 retail FX/CFD accounts is profitable. Even at the lowest-cost broker (IBKR, with commission-based pricing and many hedging and professional-style clients), fewer than half are.
- The academic evidence points to leverage, overtrading and behavioural biases as the main drivers. A low-turnover weekly system sidesteps some of those mechanisms, but none of these sources shows that long-horizon systematic retail FX trading succeeds. No documented positive base rate for "long-term retail FX systems" was found.

### Gaps
- No public dataset tracks **multi-year** performance of retail FX accounts or retail systematic FX strategies (e.g., MT4 EAs and signal services) with survivorship controls.
- No aggregate all-dealer NFA profitability figure after 2015 was retrieved, and no ESMA or FCA update after 2018 was retrieved.
- The journal publication of Heimer (2016) (cited elsewhere as *Review of Financial Studies*) was not verified from fetched pages. Heimer & Simon remained a working paper in the version read.
- The Polish KNF forex profitability survey (headline at [Finance Magnates](https://financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/exclusive-poland-financial-regulators-forex-profitability-survey-results-unmasked/amp)) was not read.

## Q5. Is there rigorous out-of-sample evidence that simple technical or factor FX rules still work after costs?

### Takeaway
The evidence has shifted clearly against simple FX rules in developed currencies after the 1990s and 2000s:
- Neely & Weller find no statistically significant positive Sharpe ratios for developed-currency technical portfolios over 2000–2012.
- Zarrabi, Snaith & Coakley find no rules surviving the stepwise-SPA test and an out-of-sample Sharpe of about 0.03 over 1996–2014.
- Hutchinson et al. (2022) find carry, momentum and value Sharpe ratios falling from +0.39 to −0.32 after publication.
- Hsu, Taylor & Wang (2016) still find data-snooping-robust profits over a 45-year, 30-currency sample, but tie them to "market immaturity," with gains varying over time and concentrated in emerging currencies.

### Cited Findings
**Hsu, Taylor & Wang (2016)**, "Technical trading: Is it still beating the foreign exchange market?", *Journal of International Economics* 102:188–208
- Tests more than 21,000 rules on 45 years of daily data for 30 developed and emerging currencies, using a stepwise test against data snooping, with out-of-sample cross-validation.
- Finds "substantial predictability and excess profitability," which varies over time and across currency groups. The authors attribute it to temporarily not-fully-rational behaviour and market immaturity.
- Source: [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/technical-trading-is-it-still-beating-the-foreign-exchange-market/)
- Gap: recent-subperiod and developed-only numbers were not retrieved.

**Neely & Weller**, "Lessons from the evolution of foreign exchange trading strategies" (St. Louis Fed WP 2011-021, revised Apr 2013)
- Average single rule: about **1.1% net per year, Sharpe 0.17**.
- Best ex-ante backtest-selected portfolio over Apr 1975 – Dec 2012: 9.40% net (10.08% gross), Sharpe 0.78. Top-10 average: 5.44% net, Sharpe 0.50.
- Over **2000–2012, developed-market-only portfolios had no statistically significant positive Sharpe ratios**.
- Top-ranked portfolios over 2003–2012 were dominated by emerging-market carry (TRY, CLP).
- Simple rule returns largely vanished after about 15 profitable years in the 1970s–80s.
- Costs were set at one-third of quoted spreads, with much higher costs for EM.
- Authors' conclusion: "trading rule returns still exist in forex markets," consistent with adaptive markets.
- Source: [St. Louis Fed WP 2011-021](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2011/2011-021.pdf)

**Zarrabi, Snaith & Coakley (2017)**, "FX technical trading rules can be profitable sometimes!", *International Review of Financial Analysis*
- Tests 7,650 rules on 6 USD pairs (GBP, CAD, JPY, SEK, CHF, NOK) over 1994–2014, with 2.5bp one-way costs.
- After false-discovery-rate control, many rules look good in-sample, but **the stepwise-SPA test finds no outperforming rules for any currency**.
- Out-of-sample portfolio Sharpe of **0.027 (1996–2014)**. Sharpe of 0.0375 over 2007–2009.
- After the crisis, outperformance disappears for all currencies except SEK.
- The authors find no persistence in profitability: opportunities are "short-lived and inconsistent."
- Source: [Essex repository accepted manuscript](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/18805/1/1-s2.0-S1057521916301958-main.pdf)

**Hutchinson, Kyziropoulos, O'Brien, O'Reilly & Sharma (2022)**, "Are carry, momentum and value still there in currencies?", *International Review of Financial Analysis* 83:102245
- Replicating the published currency carry, momentum and value studies, the **average out-of-sample Sharpe falls from +0.39 to −0.32**.
- Currencies no longer respond to interest-rate and real-exchange-rate differentials, and excess returns show no autocorrelation in the later period.
- Source: [UCC Research](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/are-carry-momentum-and-value-still-there-in-currencies-2/)

**Bartram et al. (2020)**, via Joachim Klement's summary (secondary source)
- After publication, **currency momentum profits fell by about 0.4pp per month, turning into an average monthly loss**. Value stayed around zero.
- **Carry worked only when the US dollar was excluded.**
- Combined currency-factor profits net of costs **have been negative for more than a decade since about 2008**.
- Source: [Klement on Investing, "Central banks killed currency trading"](https://klementoninvesting.substack.com/p/central-banks-killed-currency-trading)

**[Long-run background] Joseph Chen**, "Currency Investing Throughout Recent Centuries" (SSRN 4692257, rev. 2024), 21 currencies from 1788
- Carry is robust over the long run (signal-weighted Sharpe 0.391 on short rates).
- **Momentum has limited support**: it is positive only in the latter half of the sample and not robust in panel regressions.
- Value/reversal is positive only since about 1980, with a negative trend.
- Source: [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/fx-carry-value-momentum-strategies-over-their-200-year-history/)

### Inferences
- A G8 weekly momentum backtest losing money over 2000–2026 matches the published out-of-sample record. Developed-currency momentum and technical rules have had near-zero or negative risk-adjusted returns since about 2000, and especially since 2008. Making it profitable would likely require overfitting.
- The FX return that has held up best is carry, and mainly in non-USD or emerging-market crosses. It comes with crash risk (August 2024; tail-risk findings in the Dec 2011 BIS Quarterly Review), and G8 carry was thin while rates were near zero (about 2009–2021).
- Studies that still find profits (Hsu–Taylor–Wang; Neely–Weller full sample) lean on pre-2000 data and emerging currencies and assume institutional costs. Retail spreads, swap/rollover markups and MT4 execution would cut further into any edge that remains.
